# Writing

## Lead with the point

- The first paragraph says what the reader will know by the end, and why it matters to them.
- Every section opens with its conclusion, then supports it.
- Headings are statements or questions a reader could skim and still learn something ("Review agents now block merges at three vendors"), not labels ("Background").

## Plain and precise

- Use plain words. Define a term the first time it appears, in a clause, not a footnote.
- Prefer concrete to abstract: name the company, the product, the version, the number, the date.
- Avoid hype and filler: "revolutionary", "game-changer", "unprecedented", "paradigm shift", "in today's fast-moving world", "it's worth noting". If something really is unprecedented, say what it beats and cite it.
- One idea per paragraph. Paragraphs of two to five sentences.
- Active voice, and say who did it.

## Dates and numbers

- Use absolute dates ("on 9 October 2026", "in Q3 2026"). Never "recently", "last week" or "this year"; they rot.
- Numbers carry units, currency and a source. Round sensibly and say "about" when rounding.
- When comparing, give both sides of the comparison and the baseline.

## Say what kind of claim it is

Readers must be able to tell these apart at a glance:

- **Fact**: verified and sourced. State it plainly.
- **Reported**: claimed by a party with a stake (a vendor, a government, an analyst). Attribute it: "Example Corp says ..."
- **Analysis**: our reasoning from the facts. Lives in "Why it matters" sections, or is introduced with "This suggests ..." / "The likely effect is ...".
- **Forecast**: what might happen. Says so, gives the reasoning, and where possible a date by which we'd know.

## Format

- Lists for parallel items, tables for comparisons across more than two dimensions.
- At most one diagram per article, and only when it shows a mechanism prose can't. Use an image or inline SVG with alt text.
- Link to sources inline at the claim, and list them in front matter `sources`.
- Quotes are short, verbatim and linked.
- British or American spelling is fine; be consistent within an article.

## Length

Stay within the ranges in [structure.md](structure.md). Cut anything that doesn't change what the reader knows or does. No closing paragraph that repeats the TL;DR.
