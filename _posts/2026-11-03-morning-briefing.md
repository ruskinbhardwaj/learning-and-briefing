*Covers 2 to 3 November.*

**Today in one line:** two signs that AI is meeting real-world limits: a bank deciding who may use AI coding tools, and voters deciding where data centers may be built.

### Goldman Sachs limited AI coding tools to its engineers
<span class="meta">AI-native operating models</span>

Goldman Sachs has stopped traders and bankers from using some AI tools, restricting AI coding tools to its engineers ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-02/goldman-restricts-traders-and-bankers-from-using-some-ai-tools), [Quartz](https://qz.com/goldman-sachs-restricts-ai-coding-tools-traders-bankers-110226), [Finextra](https://www.finextra.com/newsarticle/48690/goldman-bans-traders-and-bankers-from-using-ai-coding-tools)). Some coverage says Anthropic's Claude Code is among the tools affected ([The State of AI](https://www.thestateofai.com/news/goldman-sachs-claude-code-traders-bankers)).

**Why it matters:** AI coding agents make it easy for anyone to build software, and that is the risk for a bank. Code written outside engineering skips review, testing and controls. Goldman's answer is to keep building with agents inside the function that owns those controls, rather than spreading it across the firm. Other regulated companies deciding how to become AI-native face the same choice.

### Data centers are on the ballot in today's US midterm elections
<span class="meta">Enabling tech</span>

Restrictions on data centers are on the ballot in dozens of US cities, according to the Detroit News ([Detroit News](https://www.detroitnews.com/story/news/politics/2026/11/02/data-center-restrictions-make-ballot-dozens-us-cities/92561478007/)). Axios reported that a backlash against AI data centers is shaping the campaign ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/31/ai-booms-political-reckoning)). Counts of how many states have related measures differ between outlets; Yahoo News put it at 10 ([Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/ai-data-centers-ballot-10-185841413.html)).

```
  Build-out needs:   chips ─▶ power ─▶ land & permits ─▶ data center
                                  ▲            ▲
                          local voters decide these
```

**Why it matters:** so far the limits on the AI build-out have been chips and money. Local politics is becoming another one. Companies can buy GPUs, but they can't build where communities vote no, and that pushes projects toward places with cheap power and friendly rules.
