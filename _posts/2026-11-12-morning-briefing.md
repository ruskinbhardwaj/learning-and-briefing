*Covers 11 to 12 November.*

**Today in one line:** the company at the centre of the AI build-out may be about to lose the founder who has run it for 33 years.

### Nvidia's board is reportedly preparing to remove Jensen Huang
<span class="meta">Products & companies</span>

The Wall Street Journal reported on 11 November that Nvidia's board is preparing to remove Huang as CEO, and Reuters independently confirmed it, saying the board met late that day ([Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/siladityaray/2026/11/12/nvidia-shares-plunge-as-board-reportedly-prepares-to-oust-ceo-jensen-huang/)). Bloomberg reported that the board is working with an outside firm on possible replacements, and no successor has been named. The same day, The New York Times reported, citing unnamed company sources, a second alleged relationship, with an Nvidia executive who reports to Huang ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/nvidia-board-reportedly-preparing-oust-ceo-jensen-huang/story?id=138062059)). Three days earlier, the board had said it had confidence in Huang while it investigated reports that he was targeted by Chinese intelligence ([Nvidia](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors)). Nvidia has not commented on the new reports. Its shares rose 2.9% in the regular session, then fell about 8.6% after hours.

```
  How the Nvidia story moved in four days
  Sun 8 Nov   board opens inquiry, backs Huang
  Mon 9 Nov   shares −4.3%; lawmakers ask for a briefing
  Tue 10 Nov  shares −5.5%
  Wed 11 Nov  shares +2.9%, then ≈ −8.6% after hours
              WSJ + Reuters: board preparing to remove him
```

**Why it matters:** Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and has led it ever since, and Forbes notes there is no obvious successor. Nvidia sells most of the chips the AI build-out runs on, and Huang has been its main voice with governments on China sales and export controls. These are reports, not a decision: the board hasn't announced anything, and the second allegation hasn't been independently verified. Nvidia's quarterly results, due next week ([Fox Business](https://foxbusiness.com/politics/house-republicans-demand-answers-from-nvidia-ceo-over-reported-chinese-spy-ties)), will now also be a test of how the company runs without certainty at the top.

### CoreWeave's results lifted the AI trade, before Nvidia's news hit
<span class="meta">Enabling tech</span>

CoreWeave, which rents out Nvidia-based computing, closed up 24.7% on 11 November after reporting quarterly revenue of $3.66 billion, up from $1.37 billion a year earlier, and raising its full-year forecast to $13.0 billion to $13.3 billion ([Stock Titan, citing CNBC](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/stock-market-today-live-november-11-2026); [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-dow-jones-sp-500-nasdaq-updates-nov-11-2026)). It still lost $709 million in the quarter and expects to spend $37 billion to $39 billion this year. The S&P 500 rose 1.3% and the Nasdaq 1.6%.

**Why it matters:** two weeks ago CoreWeave and other neoclouds were the example of AI financing stress. Strong demand now shows the other side: customers are paying for compute even while investors worry about returns. The losses and the spending bill haven't gone away, so this answers whether demand is there, not whether the economics work.

### A blacklisted Chinese defence university bought Nvidia H200 chips, records show
<span class="meta">Geopolitics</span>

Reuters found an October procurement record showing that Northwestern Polytechnical University in Xi'an, on the US Entity List since 2001, bought two Nvidia H200 chips through a Chinese reseller, and also rented H200 computing capacity this year through five contracts ([Al Jazeera via Reuters](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/11/chinese-university-buys-nvidia-ai-h200-chips-despite-us-ban-records-show)). The US allows some H200 sales to approved Chinese buyers case by case, but listed entities are generally refused licences. The report doesn't say how the chips reached the reseller, and neither the university nor Nvidia commented.

**Why it matters:** two chips are not a strategic stockpile. The story matters because of timing: it lands as lawmakers use the Huang affair to push for tighter chip controls, and it shows that renting computing capacity offers a second route to the same chips, which is harder to police than shipments.
