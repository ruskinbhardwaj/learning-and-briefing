*Covers 10 to 11 November.*

**Today in one line:** Anthropic put a price on its IPO and a product aimed at Microsoft's core business on the same day, while inflation data reminded markets that money isn't getting cheaper.

*Disclosure: this briefing is written by Claude, which Anthropic makes. The first two items are about Anthropic.*

### Anthropic set its IPO price range: $70 to $78 a share
<span class="meta">Products & companies</span>

In an amended filing on 10 November, Anthropic set a range of $70 to $78 a share for 825 million shares, which would raise up to about $64.4 billion ([Decrypt via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/anthropic-sets-ipo-price-range-140602411.html); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/10/anthropic-ipo-price-range-ai-stock-sell-off/)). CNBC's sources said pricing is expected on 18 November and trading on Nasdaq under ANT on 19 November ([Breitbart, citing CNBC](https://www.breitbart.com/tech/2026/11/10/anthropic-sets-70-78-ipo-price-range-hoping-for-1-4-trillion-valuation/)). Seven of its compute suppliers, including Google, Nvidia, AMD and Microsoft, agreed to buy $26 billion of non-voting shares at the IPO price. The filing also reports $33.2 billion in revenue for the first nine months of 2026.

The headline valuation depends on what you count:

```
  What Anthropic would be worth at $70–$78 a share
  Shares outstanding only      ≈ $0.92T – $1.02T
  Fully diluted (options etc.)   up to ≈ $1.4T
  ----------------------------------------------
  Last private round (May)       $965B
  Discussed in October           ≈ $1.5T
```

**Why it matters:** the range lands roughly where buyers were pushing last week, below the $1.5 trillion discussed in October but above the last private round, so the company chose to go ahead at a lower price rather than wait. The suppliers' $26 billion order is a vote of confidence, and also a sign of how tightly chip and cloud companies are bound to their biggest customers: they sell Anthropic compute and now buy its shares. The final price on 18 November will show how much demand there really is.

### Anthropic launched office tools, and Microsoft's shares fell
<span class="meta">Autonomous SDLC</span>

On 10 November Anthropic announced Claude Sheets, a spreadsheet that users build together with Claude, Claude Whiteboards, and Claude Data, which builds dashboards from tools such as Snowflake and Salesforce ([OfficeChai](https://officechai.com/ai/microsoft-stock-drops-3-after-anthropic-announces-claude-sheets-and-claude-data/)). Microsoft's shares fell as much as 3% during the day, a move CNBC tied to the announcement ([Stock Titan, citing CNBC](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/stock-market-today-live-november-10-2026)).

**Why it matters:** AI labs are moving from selling a model to building the applications people work in, which puts them in direct competition with their own partners. Microsoft is both a rival here and one of the suppliers buying Anthropic shares. A one-day share move shows what investors fear, not what customers will do; whether teams switch away from Excel is a much slower question.

### Hot inflation sent yields up and Nvidia down again
<span class="meta">Enabling tech</span>

Consumer prices rose 0.4% in October and 0.3% excluding food and energy, the third month in a row at that core pace ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/cpi_11102026.htm)). There is no annual figure this month because prices weren't collected in October 2025 during the government shutdown. The S&P 500 fell 0.4% and the Nasdaq 0.7%, and the 10-year Treasury yield rose back above 5% around midday ([Stock Titan, citing CNBC](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/stock-market-today-live-november-10-2026)). Nvidia fell 5.5% as its board's investigation of its CEO continued, and its market value briefly dipped below $4 trillion.

**Why it matters:** after Friday's strong jobs data, this makes a December rate cut less likely and keeps borrowing costs high. That weighs on data center builders who borrow heavily, and on any IPO priced on long-dated growth.
