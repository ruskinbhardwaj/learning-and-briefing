*Covers 24 to 25 November.*

**Today in one line:** Monday's relief rally didn't last, and Washington moved from investigating the Nvidia–China case to punishing the companies at its centre.

*Disclosure: this briefing is written by Claude, which Anthropic makes. The first and last items mention Anthropic.*

### Chip stocks gave back part of Monday's rally, and Anthropic slipped below its IPO price
<span class="meta">Enabling tech</span>

On Tuesday 24 November Nvidia fell 5.9% to $155.38, a day after its 31% jump on Jensen Huang's return, and AMD fell 8.9% to $413.27 ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/11/24/stock-market-today-nov-24-nvidia-gives-back-usd240-billion-in-market-value-despite-wall-street-upgrade-signaling-29-upside/); [The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/11/24/stock-market-today-nov-24-amd-plunges-9-as-337-million-share-resale-filing-adds-to-ai-sell-off/)). AMD had filed to register about 337 million shares for possible resale by existing holders; the filing is not an immediate sale and does not say who the holders are. The Nasdaq fell 1.5% and the S&P 500 0.5%. Anthropic traded below its $83 IPO price for the first time, around $76 to $82 during the day ([Eurasia Business News](https://eurasiabusinessnews.com/2026/11/24/stock-market-today-november-24-2026-nasdaq-falls-as-nvidia-and-anthropic-slide-oil-price-at-89-gold-drops-to-3620/)); we could not confirm its closing price.

  Thu 19  $145.98
  Fri 20  $125.53  -14%
  Mon 23  $165.05  +31%   Huang back

**Why it matters:** swings of this size in the largest chip company show the market has not settled the bigger question from last week, whether AI spending will pay off. Anthropic slipping under its IPO price in its first week is a warning for other AI companies hoping to list.

### The US blacklisted Megaspeed and an Alibaba unit over the risk of chips reaching China
<span class="meta">Geopolitics</span>

On Monday 23 November the Commerce Department added seven companies to its Entity List, including Singapore's Megaspeed, its holding company and affiliates elsewhere in South-East Asia, and Alibaba's Apex Enterprise Solutions ([PhotoNews, citing Bloomberg and AFP](https://photonews.com.pk/megaspeed-export-controls); [The Neuron](https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-november-24-2026/)). Anyone exporting US-controlled items to them now needs a licence, which will normally be refused. Commerce cited the risk the chips could be diverted to unauthorised users. Megaspeed, which bought billions of dollars of Nvidia chips, is the company at the centre of the case that briefly cost Huang his job; it has denied wrongdoing. Alibaba called the decision "erroneous" and said it would pursue legal options.

```
  Export path the Entity List now blocks
  US chip ──► Megaspeed and affiliates (South-East Asia)
                 │
                 └──x──► buyers or cloud users in China
```

**Why it matters:** this is the first concrete penalty since the Nvidia case broke, and it follows last week's rule on remote access to chips in overseas data centres. Naming an Alibaba unit takes the case beyond one company and towards China's largest cloud provider.

### Amazon is buying Decart's chip software to help it sell its own AI chips
<span class="meta">Products & companies</span>

Amazon has agreed to acquire the technology behind Decart's optimisation software, along with its founders and research team, in a deal Bloomberg puts at $7.5 billion ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/23/amazon-snaps-up-decarts-ai-optimization-tech-for-7-5b-as-it-prepares-to-sell-trainium-chips/); [The Neuron](https://www.theneuron.ai/digest/everything-that-happened-in-ai-today-november-24-2026/)). The software helps AI models run efficiently on Nvidia, Amazon and Google chips. Amazon has so far used its own Trainium chips only in its data centres and is preparing to sell them to outside customers as an alternative to Nvidia. Neither company confirmed the price.

**Why it matters:** the hardest part of competing with Nvidia is software that makes models run well on other chips. Buying that software and team is Amazon's bid to make Trainium a real alternative for others.

### The "250,000-page" Riemann proof figure was a joke, says its author
<span class="meta">Humans & AI</span>

Levent Alpöge, the Anthropic mathematician who says he and Claude proved the Riemann hypothesis, said on 23 November that a footnote claiming a 250,000-plus-page written version "was a joke", though he said long drafts did exist ([Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/anthropic-mathematician-riemann-proof-joke); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/alpoge-250000-page-riemann-proof-figure-joke-lean-clay-active)). He accepted criticism that the Lean code is badly organised and said much of the argument could probably be simplified. The proof is still not accepted by mathematicians.

**Why it matters:** the confusion shows how unready the field is for a result nobody can read. The computer check settles whether the code is consistent; whether it proves the right thing, and why it works, still needs humans.
