*Covers 4 to 5 November.*

**Today in one line:** markets read the midterms as a vote on where AI gets built, selling data center stocks and buying renewables.

### Data center stocks fell after Democrats won Congress and the Texas governorship
<span class="meta">Enabling tech</span>

Wall Street ended higher on 4 November after Democrats won both chambers of Congress, but AI data center stocks fell while renewable energy stocks rallied ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/us-stock-index-futures-steady-083456013.html), [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/ai-data-center-stocks-dip-113427491.html)). Democrat Gina Hinojosa won the Texas governor's race, and CNBC reported that investors saw the result as raising the risk of a slowdown in data center construction ([KSAT](https://www.ksat.com/news/texas/2026/11/04/gina-hinojosa-won-the-governors-race-what-happens-to-texas-data-centers-now/), [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/04/ai-stocks-texas-governor-data-centers.html)). Coverage of the Texas results pointed to voter unease about data centers ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/article/democrats-victories-in-texas-laid-bare-voter-unease-over-data-centers--and-signal-new-hurdles-for-the-industry-142918051.html)). In South Korea, home to major memory chipmakers, the Kospi swung sharply as the US results came in ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/kospi-slumps-as-democrats-us-election-lead-spurs-chip-selloff)).

**Why it matters:** Bloomberg described the AI data center boom as "set for upheaval", and Fortune argued the results will slow construction and push tech companies to negotiate with local and state governments ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-04/ai-data-center-boom-is-set-for-upheaval-after-us-election-rout), [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/04/democrats-midterm-election-2026-anti-data-center-governors-texas-georgia-ohio-ai-infrastructure-big-tech-spending-moratorium-regulations/)). Those are early readings, not policy. The new office-holders haven't said what they will do. The signal is that power and siting, not just chips and money, are now a political question for the build-out.
