*Covers 12 to 13 November.*

**Today in one line:** the face of the AI boom is out at Nvidia, and the company has five days until its results.

### Jensen Huang has left Nvidia
<span class="meta">Products & companies</span>

On 12 November Nvidia announced that Huang had resigned as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately ([Nvidia](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-CEO-Transition/)). Colette Kress, the finance chief since 2013, is interim CEO while staying CFO, and the board will look at internal and external candidates. Harvey Jones, a former Synopsys CEO, becomes Nvidia's first chair. The release gives no reason and says nothing about the board's investigation; Bloomberg described Huang as ousted by the board ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-11-12/jensen-huang-face-of-the-ai-boom-ousted-by-nvidia-board-as-ceo)). Nvidia's shares fell as much as 14% early, then closed down 4.3%, while AMD and Broadcom each rose nearly 6% ([Investopedia via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/stock-market-today-nasdaq-p-131805045.html)). The main indexes fell 0.4%, and the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.13%.

```
  Nvidia, from inquiry to exit
  Sun 8 Nov   board opens inquiry, "confidence" in Huang
  Wed 11 Nov  WSJ + Reuters: board preparing to remove him
  Thu 12 Nov  Huang resigns; Kress interim CEO; Jones chair
  Tue 17 Nov  quarterly results, "as previously scheduled"
```

**Why it matters:** Nvidia says operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap continue without interruption, and in the short run that is likely: chips already designed and ordered don't depend on one person. The longer question is strategy and influence. Huang set Nvidia's bets years ahead and was its main voice with Washington and Beijing on China sales. Rivals rising on the news suggests investors expect customers to hedge. Tuesday's results call, Kress's first as interim CEO, is the first real read.

### Congress still wants Huang's testimony
<span class="meta">Geopolitics</span>

Republican committee chairs renewed calls for Huang to testify after he resigned ([ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Politics/republicans-renew-push-jensen-huangs-testimony-nvidia-ceos/story?id=138083749)). Accounts differ on whether he has agreed. House Foreign Affairs chair Brian Mast said Huang agreed to testify before Thanksgiving, after a subpoena threat ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2315091/ex-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-is-set-to-testify-before-congress-this-month/)), while China committee chair John Moolenaar said they were still discussing a date. Democratic senators Elizabeth Warren and Chris Coons asked the administration to preserve documents on Nvidia chip exports to China.

**Why it matters:** the story is moving from one man's conduct to policy. Both parties are now asking how China sales were approved, which raises the chance of tighter export rules whoever runs Nvidia.

### Nebius cut its outlook because data centers are late
<span class="meta">Enabling tech</span>

Nebius, another company that rents out AI computing, fell 16.8% on 12 November after lowering its 2026 revenue outlook to the bottom of its $3.0 billion to $3.4 billion range ([The Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/11/12/stock-market-today-nov-12-nebius-group-sinks-nearly-17-after-lowering-2026-outlook/)). It blamed construction delays at data centers owned by others, which push revenue into 2027, and said it will build more on sites it controls. CoreWeave, up 25% the day before, fell 6.9%.

**Why it matters:** the constraint here is not demand but buildings and power. Customers want the computing; the sites aren't ready on time. That is the same bottleneck behind the midterm fights over data centers, now showing up in a company's numbers.
