*Covers 23 to 26 October, including the weekend.*

**Today in one line:** the doubts about AI spending moved from Big Tech to the companies that rent out compute, and to whether customers are seeing any return at all.

### Neoclouds came under pressure, testing Nvidia's role as their backer
<span class="meta">Enabling tech</span>

"Neoclouds" are companies that buy GPUs and rent them out as AI compute. On 23 October CNBC reported turmoil in this part of the build-out: neocloud stocks under pressure, rising credit risk, and SK hynix's storage unit Solidigm pulling its planned IPO ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/23/ai-turmoil-neoclouds-struggle-credit-risks-jump-solidigm-pulls-ipo.html)). A day later CNBC described this as the first big test of Nvidia's role as a financier of these companies, since Nvidia has invested in and backed several of its own customers ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/24/nvidias-role-as-ai-financier-faces-first-big-test-as-neoclouds-take-market-hit.html)). Nvidia's Jensen Huang had said on 22 October that neocloud demand is "off the charts" but capital is scarce ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/22/nvidia-jensen-huang-neocloud-demand-capital.html)).

```
  Nvidia ──invests / backs──▶ Neocloud ──buys GPUs──▶ Nvidia
                                 │
                       borrows to fund GPUs
                                 │
                                 ▼
                     rents compute to AI labs
```

**Why it matters:** this loop helps sell chips, but it also means stress at the neoclouds can flow back to Nvidia. Demand is not the problem Huang describes; financing is. If lenders and IPO buyers pull back, compute gets built more slowly even when customers want it.

### An Apollo economist said AI has delivered "zero financial payoff" outside tech
<span class="meta">AI-native operating models</span>

Torsten Slok, chief economist at Apollo, argued over the weekend that AI has so far produced no visible financial payoff for the S&P 500 companies outside the big tech names, the so-called S&P 493 ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-25/ai-provides-zero-payoff-to-s-p-493-outside-tech-apollo-economist-says), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/25/ai-delivers-zero-financial-payoff-to-most-companies-according-to-apollo-economist/)).

**Why it matters:** the whole spending case rests on ordinary companies, not just tech firms, eventually earning more from AI. This is the question behind "how do companies become AI-native": adopting tools is easy, while changing how work is organised so it shows up in profits is slow. Expect this argument to be tested against this week's Big Tech results.
