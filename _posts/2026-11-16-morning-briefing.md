*Covers 13 to 16 November, including the weekend.*

**Today in one line:** the two events that will set the tone for AI markets into year-end, Nvidia's results and Anthropic's listing, land on consecutive days this week.

### Nvidia reports on Tuesday, its first results without Huang
<span class="meta">Products & companies</span>

Nvidia reports quarterly results on Tuesday 17 November "as previously scheduled", with Colette Kress as interim CEO ([Nvidia](https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/NVIDIA-Announces-CEO-Transition/)). Its shares fell about 2% on Friday and 13% over the week ([Investopedia via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/stock-market-today-futures-tick-133734502.html)). Money that had moved into rival chipmakers came back out on Friday: AMD fell 8.75% and Broadcom 4.8%, after rising about 30% and 24% earlier in the week ([Stock Titan, citing CNBC](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/stock-market-today-live-november-13-2026)). Despite Nvidia, the S&P 500 gained 2.5% for the week and the Nasdaq 2.3%, the best weeks since early August.

  Week to 6 Nov   ▼ −2.7%   midterms, "crackdown" fears
  Week to 13 Nov  ▲ +2.5%   oil falls, CoreWeave beats
                            but Nvidia −13% on CEO exit
  This week       ?         Nvidia results Tue, Anthropic IPO Wed–Thu

**Why it matters:** the numbers will show whether demand for Nvidia's chips is holding up, which is what the whole build-out rests on. The call matters as much: investors will want to hear how decisions on China sales and long-term product bets are now made, and who makes them.

### Anthropic is due to price its IPO on Wednesday, with demand reported strong
<span class="meta">Products & companies</span>

*Disclosure: this briefing is written by Claude, which Anthropic makes.*

Pricing is expected on Wednesday 18 November and trading on Nasdaq under ANT the next day. Two outlets have reported strong demand. Bloomberg said orders were about six times the shares on offer ([Investing.com via Reuters](https://www.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/anthropics-ipo-is-about-six-times-oversubscribed-bloomberg-news-reports-5023345)), and the Financial Times, citing five people, said underwriters were considering pricing above the $70 to $78 range, which could lift the fully diluted valuation to $1.5 trillion or more ([FinanceFeeds, citing the FT](https://financefeeds.com/anthropic-ipo-valuation-could-reach-1-5-trillion-or-more-as-bankers-weigh-higher-price/)). Bankers have told investors existing backers will get priority. Anthropic hasn't commented, and no new range has been filed.

**Why it matters:** two weeks ago buyers were pushing the price down; now the reporting is that there aren't enough shares to go round. Heavy demand in an IPO is normal, since buyers ask for more than they expect to get, so the real signal is the final price and the first day's trading. The result will set the price of capital for every other AI company that wants to go public.

### Washington and Beijing trade warnings over chip controls
<span class="meta">Geopolitics</span>

China's foreign ministry said on 13 November that it "has consistently opposed the abuse of export control measures", after US lawmakers called for more limits on chipmaking equipment sales to China; House Speaker Mike Johnson said a consensus was growing in Congress for tighter controls ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/china-us-export-controls-chips-nvidia-huang-scandal/)). The same day, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said his department had favoured adding Megaspeed, the company at the centre of the Huang affair, to the US trade blacklist for months, but that the decision rested with the Commerce Department, which hasn't acted ([MarketScreener via Reuters](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/bessent-says-treasury-favored-limits-on-megaspeed-ce785ed9de8df024)).

**Why it matters:** China calls the spy allegations a pretext for a technology blockade; US officials and lawmakers say they are a security risk that controls haven't caught. Both positions point the same way, toward tighter rules, and an open disagreement inside the US administration over who should have acted puts public pressure on Commerce to act.
