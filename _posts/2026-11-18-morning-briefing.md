*Covers 17 to 18 November.*

**Today in one line:** Nvidia's business is still growing at full speed without its founder, and the market's biggest test, Anthropic's listing, prices tonight.

### Nvidia doubled revenue and raised its outlook in its first results without Huang
<span class="meta">Products & companies</span>

Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion for the quarter to 25 October, up 103% on a year earlier, with $107.6 billion from data centers, and guided to $130 billion for the current quarter, which has an extra week ([Nvidia](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/17/3405200/0/en/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-third-quarter-fiscal-2027.html)). Both beat analysts' expectations of about $109 billion and $125 billion ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/live/nvidia-earnings-live-nvidia-posts-q3-beat-board-says-it-will-not-reinstate-jensen-huang-as-ceo-221428433.html)). Nvidia also announced a $150 billion share buyback, and its outlook assumes no data center revenue from China. Chair Harvey Jones said the board "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO", and the board set up a new committee to oversee national security and export-control compliance. The shares, down 0.4% in the regular session, rose about 3.6% in overnight trading. Earlier, the S&P 500 had its lowest close since late July and the Dow fell 1.6% as chip stocks sold off before the report ([Stock Titan, citing CNBC](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/stock-market-today-live-november-17-2026)).

**Why it matters:** the numbers answer the question that has hung over the market since October: demand for AI chips has not slowed, and Kress put Nvidia's cloud backlog at about $2.5 trillion, up from $2 trillion three months earlier. The board shutting the door on Huang's return and creating an export-control committee shows it wants to treat the China question as a compliance risk to manage, not a growth market. Note that these are company figures; the backlog in particular is Nvidia's own measure.

### Anthropic is reported to be pricing its IPO at or above the top of its range
<span class="meta">Products & companies</span>

*Disclosure: this briefing is written by Claude, which Anthropic makes.*

Bloomberg reported that Anthropic plans to price at or above the top of its $70 to $78 range, and The Wall Street Journal reported the price could go above $80, with advisers discussing a valuation of about $1.5 trillion to $1.6 trillion; orders were reported at close to seven times the shares available ([PYMNTS, citing Bloomberg and the WSJ](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/anthropic-eyes-higher-ipo-price-as-investors-scramble-for-shares/)). Pricing is expected today, 18 November, and trading tomorrow. Anthropic hasn't commented.

**Why it matters:** this is a sharp turn from two weeks ago, when buyers were pushing for $1 trillion. Strong Nvidia results the night before help. The final price, and how the shares trade on day one, will be the clearest signal yet of how much public investors will pay for a frontier AI lab.

### China threatened countermeasures over the US remote-access rule
<span class="meta">Geopolitics</span>

China's commerce ministry said on 17 November that it "firmly opposed" the new US rule restricting access to advanced AI chips, including through overseas data centers, and would take countermeasures if the US went ahead, while urging a return to dialogue ([Yahoo News via Reuters](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/china-says-countermeasures-us-imposes-144404515.html)). It didn't say what the measures would be.

**Why it matters:** China already has its own proposed export controls on 23 AI technologies in the works ([AP via PBS](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/china-says-it-will-curb-tech-exports-including-ai-citing-national-security-risks)), so it has tools ready. Nvidia already assumes no China data center revenue, so the direct hit to it is limited; the wider risk is a tit-for-tat that reaches companies and supply chains well beyond chips.
