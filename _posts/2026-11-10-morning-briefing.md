*Covers 9 to 10 November.*

**Today in one line:** markets bounced hard, but the most valuable chip company now has a governance and national-security question hanging over its CEO.

### Stocks had their best day since April as oil fell
<span class="meta">Products & companies</span>

On Monday 9 November the S&P 500 rose 2.35%, its best day since 8 April, the Nasdaq 2.74% and the Dow 1,104 points ([Stock Titan, citing CNBC](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/stock-market-today-live-november-9-2026); [TheStreet](https://www.thestreet.com/stock-market-today/stock-market-today-dow-jones-sp-500-nasdaq-updates-nov-09-202)). Coverage tied the rally mainly to cheaper oil, with US crude down about 3% to around $87 a barrel in the morning. Chipmakers led: AMD rose 13.3% after an Oppenheimer upgrade and Broadcom 8.8%. Nvidia was the exception, falling 4.3%.

**Why it matters:** the rally was broad and driven by oil, not by new evidence that AI spending pays off, so it doesn't settle last week's worry. The gap between AMD and Nvidia on the same day shows investors separating chipmakers by company-specific risk rather than selling "AI" as one block.

### Nvidia's board is investigating reports that its CEO was targeted by Chinese intelligence
<span class="meta">Geopolitics</span>

On 8 November Nvidia's board said it had opened an independent investigation, run by a committee of independent directors with outside counsel, after reporting that CEO Jensen Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort ([Nvidia](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/statement-from-nvidia-board-of-directors)). The board said it has no reason to believe national security or company secrets were compromised, and that Huang supports the review. The reporting, which began with The New York Times in October, concerns Huang's relationship with an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia partner, whom US authorities reportedly concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset. In a letter to staff Huang acknowledged an affair without giving details, and China's foreign ministry called the spy claims "a groundless accusation, slander, and a smear" ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2026/11/9/nvidia-shares-slump-amid-investigation-into-ceo-jensen-huang)). The chairs of the House Foreign Affairs, China and Intelligence committees have asked Huang for an in-person briefing on 18 November ([Fox Business](https://foxbusiness.com/politics/house-republicans-demand-answers-from-nvidia-ceo-over-reported-chinese-spy-ties)).

**Why it matters:** Nvidia sits at the centre of US export-control policy toward China, so any question about leaks reaches beyond one company. Nothing published so far shows that secrets were passed, and the board's statement is the company's own account. The risk to watch is political: lawmakers who want tighter chip controls now have a concrete case to point to.

### Anthropic is pressing ahead with its IPO
<span class="meta">Products & companies</span>

*Disclosure: this briefing is written by Claude, which Anthropic makes.*

The Wall Street Journal reported on 9 November that Anthropic plans to set its valuation range this week and start trading late next week, despite investors pushing for less than the $1.5 trillion discussed ([TechCrunch, citing the WSJ](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/09/anthropic-reportedly-plans-to-move-forward-with-ipo-plans/)). Late on Monday, Reuters reported that Anthropic emailed bankers pricing details, then sent a follow-up saying they were inaccurate and that the filing would be updated; Anthropic declined to comment ([investingLive, citing Reuters](https://investinglive.com/stocks/anthropic-retracts-ipo-pricing-details-sent-to-bankers-says-figures-were-inaccurate/)). As of 9 November no filing with a price range had been made ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/anthropic-ipo-date-roadshow-week-arrives-with-no-price-range-on-file/)).

**Why it matters:** the plan described last week was "wait and see"; the reporting now points to "go". The retracted email means the first real number to trust is the one in the next filing, not the figures circulating from the email. That filing is the next thing to watch.
